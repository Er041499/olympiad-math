    A learning path in four stages

    Move on when you can solve most problems of a stage without hints.

    1. Build the toolkit

      School algebra, divisibility rules, basic counting and angle facts. Solve many short problems.

    2. Learn the classic ideas

      Pigeonhole, induction, modular arithmetic, AM–GM. One idea per week, five problems each.

    3. Write full proofs

      Practice explaining every step. A correct idea with a gap in the proof loses points.

    4. Mix and compete

      Work through past national contests under time limits, then review every mistake.

    Practice problems

    Try each problem on paper first. Open the solution only after a real attempt.

    Remainders of squares Beginner · Number theory

    Problem. Prove that a perfect square leaves remainder 0 or 1 when divided by 4.

    Solution. An integer is either 2k or 2k+1. Then (2k)² = 4k², remainder 0. And (2k+1)² = 4k² + 4k + 1 = 4(k² + k) + 1, remainder 1.

    Two people with equal handshakes Beginner · Combinatorics

    Problem. At a party of 10 or more people, show that at least two people shook the same number of hands.

    Solution. With n people, handshake counts range from 0 to n−1. But 0 and n−1 cannot both occur, since someone who shook everyone’s hand rules out someone who shook none. So only n−1 values are available for n people, and the pigeonhole principle gives a repeat.

    A first inequality Intermediate · Algebra

    Problem. For positive a and b, prove that a/b + b/a ≥ 2.

    Solution. Multiply by ab: the claim becomes a² + b² ≥ 2ab. This is equivalent to (a − b)² ≥ 0, which is always true.

    Angle in a semicircle Beginner · Geometry

    Problem. Point C lies on a circle with diameter AB. Prove that angle ACB is 90°.

    Solution. Let O be the center. Triangles OAC and OBC are isosceles, so the base angles are equal: call them x and y. The angles of triangle ABC sum to x + y + (x + y) = 180°, so x + y = 90°, which is angle ACB.
